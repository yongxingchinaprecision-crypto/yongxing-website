Custom Stainless Steel Investment Casting: RFQ Checklist for U.S. Buyers

What to send a supplier so your custom metal component can be reviewed and quoted more clearly.

A request for quotation (RFQ) is the starting point for evaluating a custom investment casting project. Clear technical and commercial information helps the buyer and manufacturer identify open questions before sampling or production. This checklist is intended for U.S. OEM purchasing teams sourcing custom stainless steel components.

1. Part definition: drawing and model

  • Attach the latest 2D engineering drawing, including units and revision.
  • Include a 3D CAD model when available.
  • If you are working from a physical sample, describe the sample and note whether a drawing can be developed.
  • Identify the part number, intended function and how it interfaces with other components.

2. Material and service conditions

Specify the required stainless steel grade and governing material standard whenever known. Also describe the component’s service environment—for example, exposure to moisture, cleaning chemicals, temperature or other conditions relevant to material selection. If the grade is not yet determined, state the performance requirements and ask the supplier to review possible options. Confirm the selected grade before production.

3. Quantity and commercial assumptions

  • Initial order quantity and estimated annual demand
  • Expected order frequency or forecast, if available
  • Whether tooling or pattern costs should be quoted separately
  • Requested quotation basis and delivery destination
  • Target timing for samples, approval and production

These details can affect tooling, process planning, unit pricing and lead-time estimates. Ask the supplier to state assumptions and exclusions in the quotation.

4. Critical dimensions and tolerances

Mark critical-to-function dimensions, datum references, fit requirements and any dimensions that need special inspection. Avoid applying a tight tolerance to every dimension unless the design requires it. Tolerances should be reviewed against the component geometry, casting process and any secondary machining. Confirm achievable tolerances for the specific part rather than assuming one universal value.

5. Surface finish and secondary operations

Describe the required appearance and functional finish. Identify whether machining, polishing, deburring or other finishing is required, and indicate the areas that need it. If a surface roughness value or visual standard applies, include the specification and how it will be evaluated. A photo or approved reference sample can help clarify appearance expectations.

6. Quality, inspection and documentation

List the inspection criteria that matter for acceptance, such as dimensional checks, material verification, visual inspection or project-specific tests. State whether a material certificate, inspection report, sample approval or other documentation is required. Any certification or testing requirement should be discussed and confirmed before placing the order; do not assume it is included automatically.

7. Packaging and delivery

Explain packaging needs, protection for finished surfaces, labeling, quantity per package and any export or receiving requirements. Provide the delivery location and target schedule so the supplier can quote against a clear shipping assumption.

RFQ checklist at a glance

  • ☐ 2D drawing and revision; 3D model if available
  • ☐ Material grade / standard or performance requirements
  • ☐ Application and service environment
  • ☐ Initial quantity and annual demand
  • ☐ Critical dimensions, tolerances and datums
  • ☐ Surface finish, machining and polishing details
  • ☐ Inspection criteria and requested documents
  • ☐ Tooling, sampling and approval expectations
  • ☐ Packaging, delivery destination and target schedule
