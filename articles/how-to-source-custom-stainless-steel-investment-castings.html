How to Source Custom Stainless Steel Investment Castings

A practical checklist for purchasing teams preparing a custom metal component project.

When sourcing a custom stainless steel investment casting, a clear request for quotation (RFQ) helps both the buyer and manufacturer evaluate the project. The drawing is important, but material, production quantity, finishing and inspection requirements also affect the quotation and manufacturing plan.

This guide outlines the information an OEM purchasing team can prepare before contacting a supplier.

1. Share the latest part drawing

Provide a current 2D drawing and, when available, a 3D model. Identify critical dimensions and any features that require special attention. If the project is based on a sample or an existing component, explain what reference information is available and whether a new drawing can be supplied.

Make sure the files represent the intended revision. If a design is still changing, indicate which dimensions or features are not yet final.

2. Specify the material requirement

“Stainless steel” describes a family of materials rather than one exact grade. State the required grade or material specification when it is known. If the grade has not yet been selected, share the component’s application and operating environment so the material choice can be reviewed.

For custom castings, material suitability should be confirmed against the customer’s specification and the part’s actual service conditions.

3. Explain quantity and order expectations

Include the estimated annual demand, expected quantity per order and whether the project is for sampling, pilot production or ongoing supply. These details help a supplier understand the production context and discuss tooling and manufacturing arrangements.

If the forecast is uncertain, provide a reasonable range and identify the assumptions behind it.

4. Describe finishing and machining needs

Some cast components require additional operations after casting. Specify whether the part needs machining, polishing or another surface treatment. For visible or functional surfaces, indicate the required finish and any areas where appearance or dimensional control is especially important.

Where possible, mark these requirements directly on the drawing rather than relying only on a general note.

5. Define inspection and acceptance criteria

Tell the supplier which dimensions are critical, what inspection records are required and how the finished parts will be accepted. If your company has a quality specification or inspection plan, include it with the RFQ.

It is useful to agree on the inspection approach before production begins. Specific tolerances and inspection methods should be confirmed for each drawing and project.

6. Ask for a feasibility review

Before placing an order, ask the manufacturer to review the drawing for casting feasibility and identify any features that may require design adjustment, tooling consideration or secondary machining. A technical discussion early in the process can help clarify expectations.

Do not assume that every geometry, tolerance or surface requirement is achievable without review. Confirm the details for the individual component.

RFQ checklist for custom cast components

  • Latest 2D drawing and available 3D model
  • Material grade or specification
  • Application and operating environment
  • Annual forecast and order quantity
  • Required machining, polishing or surface finish
  • Critical dimensions, tolerances and inspection criteria
  • Sample requirements and target project timing
In short: A well-prepared RFQ gives the manufacturer a clearer basis to evaluate the component, discuss feasibility and provide a meaningful quotation.
