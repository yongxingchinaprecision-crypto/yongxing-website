What Is Stainless Steel Investment Casting?

A practical overview of the process and the details buyers should consider when sourcing custom metal components.

Stainless steel investment casting is a metal-forming process used to produce components with detailed shapes and carefully controlled dimensions. It is also known as lost-wax casting. The method can be useful when a part’s geometry is difficult or costly to make through other manufacturing routes.

For OEM purchasing teams: The right process depends on the component drawing, material requirements, quantity, surface finish and inspection criteria. A supplier should review these details before confirming feasibility.

How the investment casting process works

A typical investment casting workflow includes several stages:

  1. Pattern and tooling preparation. A pattern representing the component is produced, often using a dedicated mold.
  2. Wax pattern assembly. Patterns are assembled to form a casting tree.
  3. Ceramic shell building. The assembly is coated with ceramic material to create a shell.
  4. Wax removal and shell preparation. The wax is removed and the shell is prepared for pouring.
  5. Metal pouring. Molten metal is poured into the prepared shell.
  6. Shell removal and finishing. After solidification, the ceramic shell is removed; castings are separated and finished.
  7. Inspection. The finished parts are checked against the agreed drawing and quality requirements.

Why stainless steel is used for custom components

Stainless steel is selected for many components where corrosion resistance, strength, appearance or service conditions matter. The suitable grade depends on the operating environment and the customer’s specification. Buyers should identify the required grade rather than relying on a general description such as “stainless steel.”

What buyers should provide when requesting a quotation

To help a manufacturer evaluate a custom casting project, purchasing teams can prepare:

  • 2D drawings and, if available, a 3D model
  • Required stainless steel grade or material specification
  • Expected annual quantity and order batch size
  • Critical dimensions, tolerances and inspection requirements
  • Surface finish, polishing or machining requirements
  • Application details and relevant operating conditions

Is investment casting right for every part?

Not necessarily. The most suitable process should be evaluated against the component’s geometry, material, production volume, dimensional requirements and total project cost. Some parts may need additional machining or finishing after casting. A technical review based on the drawing is the practical starting point.
